Trust is confidence that another person or system will act reliably enough for cooperation.

When trust is high, people can move faster, share more, and take risks together. When trust is low, every action requires verification, protection, and negotiation.

This is as true in software as it is in relationships.

A customer trusts a system when it behaves consistently, explains what happened, and gives them a recovery path when something goes wrong. A teammate becomes trustworthy by making honest commitments and communicating before surprises become failures. A relationship deepens when repeated actions make care believable.

Trust is not naivety. Different people, systems, and institutions carry different incentives and failure modes. Mature trust leaves room for evidence, boundaries, and repair.

The important part is repetition. One impressive act can create attention. Trust usually comes from many ordinary moments in which the promise and the behavior match.

That is why trust compounds, while a single betrayal can destroy years of accumulated confidence.
