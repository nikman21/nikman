I woke up feeling groggy and unmotivated. I made coffee because I thought it would give me the energy I needed to conquer the day.

It did not.

I lay down and started scrolling through TikTok without thinking. When I got up to use the bathroom, I was already planning to come back and keep scrolling. Then I stopped at the balcony and decided to see what the weather felt like.

The air was beautiful.

It was like stepping outside broke a spell. I remember thinking: get up. Go outside. Stop sitting in this depressive cave and waiting to feel different.

I put on workout clothes and finally started my morning routine. I meditated for five minutes. I prayed and asked God for the strength to do what I needed to do that day. Then I laced up my shoes and began collecting the usual equipment for a run: headphones, phone, Apple Watch.

The watch was dead.

I grabbed my phone and headphones anyway. I was almost out the door when I stopped again and wondered whether I needed any of it.

So I left everything at home.

I am not completely sure why I made that choice. Part of me believes God may have been trying to get my attention. I do not want to state that as a certainty. I only know that the decision felt important once I was outside.

Without music, I heard birds and wind. I noticed the rhythm of my breathing instead of a pace number. The brain fog began to clear, and the run felt less like exercise I had to complete and more like freedom.

I felt like a kid again, a kid who had not learned to fill every quiet moment with technology.

I started seeing things I normally passed without noticing. Birds moved through the trees. Ants worked along the ground. The river kept moving. Deer, birds, insects, and people were all sharing the same place, each trying to make it through another day.

None of those things had suddenly appeared. I had simply become available to notice them.

As I ran, I started thinking about who I am, what I believe, and what I want to build. The absence of constant input created room for my own thoughts to return. I knew I needed to write about the experience as soon as I got home.

I do not think a walk or run in nature automatically cures depression, anxiety, stress, or burnout. That would be too simple, and it would turn one morning into a promise it cannot make.

But I learned something true about myself that day.

When I feel trapped inside noise, going outside without technology can help me become present again. It gives my attention somewhere real to land: wind, water, trees, animals, prayer, breath, and the ordinary world continuing without asking me to scroll through it.

The world had been there all morning.

I just needed to leave my phone at home long enough to see it.
