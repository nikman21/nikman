I woke up groggy and unmotivated. I made coffee, hoping it would help, but I still felt the same.

I lay down and started scrolling through TikTok without thinking. When I got up to use the bathroom, I was already planning to come back and keep scrolling. Then I stopped at the balcony and decided to see what the weather felt like.

The air was beautiful. I remember thinking: get up. Go outside. Stop sitting in this depressive cave and waiting to feel different.

I put on workout clothes and finally started my morning routine. I meditated for five minutes. I prayed and asked God for the strength to do what I needed to do that day. Then I laced up my shoes and began collecting the usual equipment for a run: headphones, phone, Apple Watch.

The watch was dead, so I grabbed my phone and headphones. I was almost out the door when I stopped again and wondered whether I needed any of it. I left everything at home.

I’m not sure why I made that choice. Part of me believes God may have been trying to get my attention. I can’t say for certain, but the decision felt important once I was outside.

Without music, I heard birds and wind. I noticed the rhythm of my breathing instead of a pace number. The brain fog began to clear, and the run felt less like exercise I had to complete and more like freedom.

I felt like a kid again, before I’d learned to fill every quiet moment with technology.

I started seeing things I normally passed without noticing. Birds moved through the trees. Ants worked along the ground. The river kept moving. Deer, birds, insects, and people were all sharing the same place, each trying to make it through another day.

As I ran, I started thinking about who I am, what I believe, and what I want to build. Without something playing in my ears, I had room to follow my own thoughts. I wanted to write about the experience as soon as I got home.

I don’t take that morning as a cure for depression, anxiety, stress, or burnout. But when I feel stuck scrolling, going outside without my phone can help me pay attention again. That day I had birds and wind to listen to, and time to think and pray while I ran.
